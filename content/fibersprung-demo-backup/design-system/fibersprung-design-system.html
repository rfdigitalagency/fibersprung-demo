RF Digital · Design System · v1.4

FiberSprung

A patented fiberglass sprung floor sold to universities, professional companies, and theaters. This system exists to make a technical product look inevitable rather than merely competent.

The organizing idea

Light is the surface. Dark is the structure underneath.

Every light section of this site is the floor you stand on: finished, calm, ready. Every dark section is what's beneath it: the lattice, the engineering, the reason it lasts thirty years. Colour is not decoration here, it tells the reader where they are in the product.

Why not a dark site with a green accent. That is the obvious answer to this logo, and it is the wrong one. It buries a product whose whole promise is lightness and longevity, and it looks like every other engineered-materials site. The palette below is drawn from the materials in FiberSprung's own installation photographs — graphite lattice, concrete subfloor, birch plywood, maple — not from the logo. Green is a signal, used perhaps six times on the entire page.

Feel

  • Engineered, not clinical. Confident, not loud.
  • Spacious. Air is the primary luxury signal.
  • Precise motion. Slow, eased, never bouncy.
  • The photography carries the beauty. The interface stays quiet.

Never

  • Green floods, green gradients, green buttons everywhere.
  • Stock imagery in any technical section.
  • Rounded, friendly, soft. This is a manufacturer.
  • Decoration that isn't derived from the product.

Signal — the brand green. Bright green is not legible as text on light; use the deep value for type and reserve the bright value for marks, rules, dots, and on-dark use.

Green#70AB41 · marks only
Green deep#486D29 · 4.56:1 on both
Green glow#7EBC4E · 7.45:1 on dark

Sampled and locked. #70AB41, taken from the logo field — hue 93°, saturation 45%. It is far more olive and far less acid than it reads on the trade-show banner, which is fortunate: it behaves like a considered brand colour rather than a highlighter. At 2.33:1 on the light base it cannot carry text, so --green-deep (#486D29, 5.06:1 on concrete and 4.56:1 on concrete-deep) does all typographic work on light and --green-glow (#7EBC4E, 7.45:1) does it on dark. The brand value itself is reserved for marks, rules, and dots.

v1.2 — green-deep darkened. It was #496F2A, measured at 4.92:1 against --concrete. That figure only ever held on the page base: against --concrete-deep, the alternating band, the same value gives 4.43:1 and fails the 4.5 floor. Nothing had used the alternating band as a background for green text until sections 08 and 11 did. #486D29 holds the sampled 93° hue and the saturation, drops lightness about two percent, and clears AA on every light surface in the system: 5.06:1 on concrete, 4.56:1 on concrete-deep, 5.79:1 on paper. The locked brand value #70AB41 is untouched — --green-deep is a derived working colour, not the sampled one.

The signature element

The lattice rule

One ornament, derived directly from the product, used consistently. A hairline with connector blocks at the intersections — a fragment of their own grid, abstracted. It separates sections, underlines eyebrows, and at very low opacity and large scale it becomes the only background texture permitted on dark panels.

  • Section dividers, full width, graphite on light and green-glow at 55% on dark.
  • Never more than one lattice rule per screen height.
  • As background texture: the same motif tiled, 3–4% opacity, dark panels only, never behind body text.
  • It is the only decorative device in the system. Nothing else is added.

Motion

Slow, eased, engineered

Motion here should feel like a well-machined drawer closing. Nothing springs, nothing bounces, nothing overshoots. Precision is the brand attribute being expressed.

  • Animate transform and opacity only. Never width, height, top, or margin.
  • Stagger sibling reveals by 80–120ms. Never more than four items in a stagger.
  • Scroll reveals travel a short distance: 16–24px. Long travel reads as cheap.
  • prefers-reduced-motion renders the final state instantly. Non-negotiable.
  • Below 900px, drop scroll-scrubbing entirely and play reveals once on entry via IntersectionObserver.

The one orchestrated moment is the hero: the frame pins, scales from 58% to full bleed, the headline rises, then the next section climbs over it. Everything else on the page is quiet by comparison. Spend the boldness once. The mechanic is specified in scroll-mechanic-reference.html.

Image treatment

One grade holds it together

The source photographs are installer phone shots across four different lighting conditions. Left alone they read as a scrapbook. Graded to a single cool near-monochrome, they read as an authored body of work. This grading pass is the difference between "he used our Facebook photos" and "he made our work look intentional."

Technical layer — grid, process, install

Desaturate to 12–18%, cool the shadows slightly, lift black point marginally so it never crushes. Consistent across every image in these sections.

filter: saturate(.15) contrast(1.06) brightness(1.02);

Payoff layer — finished floors, people

Full colour, gently warmed. The return of colour is the emotional beat: you have come back up from under the floor to the room where the work happens.

filter: saturate(1.02) contrast(1.03);

Grade assignment by section. Technical — 04, 05, 06. Payoff — 03, 08, 09, 10, 11. Hero (01) carries no filter and is the deliberate exception. Sections 02 and 07 have no imagery. Section 11 was added to the payoff list v1.1 — its texture break is react-edge-macro.jpg, the same product from the same shoot as the two React swatches in section 08. The same subject appearing twice on one page under two different treatments is the kind of inconsistency a careful eye catches without being able to name it. Every section now has an assigned grade; none are left to be inferred.

  • The hero (maple + lattice) is the deliberate exception: keep its warmth at full strength. It is the only warm image above the fold.
  • No image sits at full bleed unless it is sharp at 2000px+. Soft images are contained, never enlarged.
  • Every image carries descriptive alt text naming the institution where known.
  • Serve AVIF with WebP and JPEG fallbacks. Hero preloaded, never lazy-loaded.

Quality floor

Non-negotiable

Structure & search

  • One H1 per page, logical H2/H3 beneath it.
  • Semantic landmarks: header, nav, main, section, footer.
  • Unique meta title and description per page, written by hand.
  • Descriptive alt text on every image.
  • Descriptive URLs, XML sitemap, Search Console verified.
  • Organization schema covering both facilities.

Access & performance

  • 4.5:1 contrast minimum on all text. Bright green never carries text on light.
  • Visible keyboard focus on every interactive element.
  • Reduced motion fully respected.
  • Hero preloaded, not lazy-loaded. LCP under 2.5s.
  • Responsive to 360px. Scroll-scrub disabled below 900px.
  • No layout shift. Explicit dimensions on every image.
