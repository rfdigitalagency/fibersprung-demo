### The bend test — *the centerpiece of this section*

```
H3        We tested it against wood.

BODY      To back up our math, we bend tested the wood of a wooden sprung floor
          against our Pro floor fiberglass, which was designed to exactly mimic
          its bend characteristics.

DATA      10           Support beams tested
DATA      2            Independent suppliers
DATA      1×4 pine     The wood typically used in sprung floors
DATA      3 ft         Test span

RESULT-A  Wood
          Deflected between 3mm and 7mm depending on the grain. Sometimes one
          side of the same beam bent more than the other.

RESULT-B  FiberSprung fiberglass
          Bent 3mm. Every single time.
```

*Reproduce these figures exactly. This is their strongest technical proof and it is

## 06 · PROCESS

```
EYEBROW   How a floor gets made

H2        Five steps, every time.
```

```
01  Design and planning
    All FiberSprung floor designs are based in Computer Aided Design. Floors are
    manufactured from the CAD files to quality tolerances defined by drawings,
    ensuring consistency across every part. Pre-planned layouts mean lean
    efficiency, less waste, and cost savings in time and materials.

02  Pre-assembly and delivery
    A ~6 week lead time begins once an order is placed. Fiberglass components are
    built and pre-assembled with FiberSprung fittings, and every piece of plywood
    and fiberglass joint is CNC machined for a consistently perfect fit. Every
    quotation includes installation — our own installers travel to your site.

03  The fiberglass grid
    The basket-weave support structure goes down first. This is where the spring
    comes from, and where the 20 year warranty applies.

04  The support surface
    A single layer of 3/4" or 1" plywood. Every joint is tongue-and-grooved, then
    fastened on both sides to a fiberglass plate — supporting the joint from
    beneath and producing a homogeneous-feeling surface. Single layer construction
    is a deliberate choice: it makes repair fast when a floor is damaged.

05  The performance surface
    Typically marley for dance, with thin engineered hardwood and vinyl also
    available. Installing the surface is the finishing touch of our service.
```

## 09 · ORIGIN

```
EYEBROW   Our story

H2        It started with a sprinkler.

BODY      The idea for FiberSprung came when Spencer Hering lost a wood-sprung
          dance floor to a sprinkler system malfunction. Afterwards, Hurricanes
          Harvey, Irma, and Maria destroyed a plethora of sprung floors in Texas,
          Florida, and Puerto Rico in the summer of 2017, highlighting the need
          for a flood-proof flooring system. Spencer had built dance floors
          before, and got to thinking there had to be a better way.

          On his final day as a professional dancer, Spencer brought the idea to
          product designer Micah Reyes, whose background is composites design
          through aerospace and yacht design. They quickly realized the potential
          of composites like fiberglass to increase lifetime, improve consistency,
          and improve the quality of sprung floors. That is when research and
          development for FiberSprung began.

          FiberSprung uses high performance, long lasting composites in place of
          the wood inside basket-weave sprung floors. Using structural
          engineering, our floors match the bend characteristics of a traditional
          sprung floor — and we developed and patented an elastomer and aluminum
          fitting method that makes the fiberglass lattice modular and reusable.
```

*The 2017 hurricane detail is the whole water-resistance argument told as a story
rather than a claim. Do not cut it.*

## EDITORIAL NOTES

**What was cut and why.** The email signup (never used), the standalone Coming Soon
block (folded into the range), the Ask AI button, and the Resale page (empty — but
resale survives as a benefit in the comparison table and the thesis).

**What must not change.** The bend test figures. The comparison table. All product
heights, install times, and named users. Patent numbers. "Day 1 to Day 3001." The
kitchen sponge line. The 2017 hurricane detail. The team's third descriptors.

**Tone.** Their existing writing is plain, confident, and occasionally funny. It does
not oversell. Anywhere this document tightened a sentence, it removed words rather
than adding adjectives. Resist the urge to make it sound more like marketing — the
restraint is what makes an engineering claim believable.
